Handshake-phase connections are cut at the deadline. Verified with Autobahn|Python on both transports.
- [ ] Load-aware connection placement: the acceptor picks the shard with the fewest sessions, so an idle and a busy session weigh the same and a hot connection can overload one shard while others idle. - [ ] Per-session memory (investigated on macOS with `heap`, 5,000 idle established sessions on a plain-TCP router: 6.7 KB per session, all of it live heap, not kernel buffers). What it is made of, per session: `WampSession` 0.5 KB, its event channel 0.2 KB plus about 0.2 KB of channel/cancellation handlers, the reader/writer `parallel_group` state 0.24 KB, one 1.5 KB block (a coroutine frame, probably the writer's or the session's), and about 25 other small Asio handler/coroutine allocations (16-336 B) that are inherent to the reader/writer coroutine structure. Done: the writer no longer reserves its 65-entry WebSocket header array (about 1.5 KB of address space per session) for plain CBOR RawSocket sessions. Remaining ideas, each worth under 1 KB: find which coroutine owns the 1.5 KB frame and move rarely used locals out; share one `parallel_group` for reader and writer; lazily create the channel until the first event or invocation. The EC2 growth from 5.5 KB was not reproduced separately (macOS 7.0 KB before, 6.7 KB after the reserve fix).
- [x] Unpaced fan-out at 2 router threads lost 0-8% of deliveries: found and fixed. It was real loss, not lateness: the cross-shard inbox overflowed its 65,536 cap (`inbox_dropped` on the receiving shard, up to 125k per run). Cause: `Shard::congested` (the shards a publish filled) belongs to the shard, but `handle_socket_read_completion` read it only after `deliver_blocked` had suspended the session, and another session's `process()` clears it; with full subscriber queues (default 100) the publisher's signal was lost, so it was not slowed. Fix: take the list right after `process()`, before any suspension (`include/wamp_server.hpp`). EC2 (router on 2 pinned cores, 4 x 7 `wamp_load` processes, 28 topics x 10 subscribers, 11.2M deliveries per run, default queue size): before, 3 of 4 runs lost 1-4% (all as `inbox_dropped`; the old 1 s cutoff showed 3-10% short); with `--queue-size 1000` 4 of 4 runs lost nothing (the control that pointed at blocked subscribers); with the fix 5 of 5 runs delivered everything with zero drops. `wamp_load` gained a per-subscriber sequence check and `--idle-cutoff`.
- [ ] Idle-shard wake-up cost: a cross-shard hand-off to a sleeping shard thread costs about 0.2-0.3 ms (EC2). Option: a configurable spin-before-sleep window (`idle_spin_us`, default 0) that polls the `io_context` briefly after work; costs CPU and cgroup quota, so only if a latency-sensitive workload needs it.
